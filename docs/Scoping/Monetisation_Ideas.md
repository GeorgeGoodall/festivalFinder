# Monetisation Ideas

## High-fit options (aligned with MVP)

### 1. Affiliate ticket links
Sign up for affiliate programs with ticket platforms (Ticketmaster, Skiddle, SeeTickets, Dice). Earn a commission on every ticket sold through outbound links. Lowest-friction option — just swap `ticket_url` links for affiliate ones.

### 2. Featured/promoted festival listings
Charge festival organizers to pin their festival at the top of search results or feature it on the homepage. The homepage already has a concept of featured festivals — this is a natural paid placement.

### 3. Display ads
Run Google AdSense or a festival-niche ad network once there is traffic. Festival-goers are a valuable demographic (18-35, disposable income). Low effort but requires meaningful traffic volume first.

## Medium-fit options (small extensions needed)

### 4. Festival organizer dashboard (SaaS)
Charge organizers a fee to claim their festival page and manage their listing — update lineups, add special offers, respond to questions, see analytics on page views. Think "Google Business Profile" for festivals.

### 5. Sponsored content / email newsletter
Build a mailing list (e.g., "festivals matching your taste this month") and sell sponsorship slots. Festival organizers pay to be featured in the email.

### 6. "Festival finder" premium tier
Free: basic search and filters. Paid (small annual fee): personalised recommendations via Spotify integration, alerts when artists you follow are added to festivals, price drop notifications.

## Recommended starting point

**Affiliate ticket links** — requires almost zero code changes (just format the `ticket_url` as an affiliate link), monetises from day one, and aligns with user intent (they want to buy tickets). Pair with **featured listings** once there are enough festivals in the database to make placement valuable.
